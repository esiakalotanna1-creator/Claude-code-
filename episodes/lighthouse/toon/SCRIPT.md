# THE LIGHT AT SEA — script

**Format:** 45 s, 9:16, one location (a lantern room), one character (Ned, the keeper). Fictional story, labelled
"A SHORT STORY" over the first seconds.

## Three hooks (the pick is #2)

1. "For thirty years, the keeper logged a ship that never existed."
2. **"Every night at dusk, the keeper flashes his lamp three times." → "Tonight, something out at sea flashed back."**
3. "The sea answered the lighthouse."

**Pick:** #2 — an ordinary ritual stated first, then one specific break in it; the viewer wants to know what flashed
back. #1 gives away the logbook, #3 has no specific image.

## Narration (≈ 85 words, read at an unhurried pace)

Every night at dusk, the keeper flashes his lamp three times.
Tonight, something out at sea flashed back.
Same rhythm. Same count. Where no ship sails.
He flashed twice. It answered twice.
He flashed once. It came closer.
Closer, every time, until it hung outside the glass.
He opened the logbook. Thirty years of entries.
Every night, the same line: Light seen. Answered.
The light wasn't copying him.
He had always been copying it.
Tonight, he kept his hand still.
The lamp flashed anyway.
Three times.

## Retention edit

- The opening raises one specific question (what flashed back?) and the next line develops it.
- Setup is 5 s; the rest is escalation. Every ~2 s something changes: a new flash pattern, the light closer, the book,
  the reversal.
- Cut candidates if a voice take runs long: "Same rhythm. Same count." (keep "Where no ship sails."), "Closer, every
  time,".
- The reveal ("He had always been copying it") lands at 31 s; the last 14 s pay it off visually with no extra
  explanation.
