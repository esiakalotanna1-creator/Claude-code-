# LEAVE A MESSAGE: script

**Format:** about 2 min 30 s, 9:16. Three characters (Maya, Theo, Frank), two homes at night in the same rainy city,
plus one warm memory. Not horror: a quiet, true-feeling story with a turn at the end. Narrator plus four short voiced
lines (Frank's voicemail greeting, Theo, Maya), the rest is on phone screens.

## Three hooks (the pick is #1)

1. **"For eleven months, Maya called her father every night at eleven. He had been dead for all of them."**
2. "Twenty-one missed calls. Same number. Always eleven o'clock. Never a message."
3. "The voicemail greeting was the only recording of her dad's voice she had left."

**Pick #1:** a specific, unsettling contradiction that the story fully explains (she calls to hear his voicemail).
#2 is the best second beat (Theo's side), so it is kept there. #3 explains too much too early.

## Story in one line

A grieving woman calls her late father's number every night to hear his voicemail; the number has been given to a
lonely sixteen-year-old, and instead of shutting her out, he becomes the person who finally calls her back.

## Retention edit

- The opening raises one question (why call a dead man?) and line 3 answers it at once, which raises the next
  (who picked up?). Every 5–8 s a new piece of information or a decision lands.
- The middle moves: the montage is cut to three images, and the memory is one shot.
- The turn (the new greeting) is set up by lines 23–25 and pays off without explanation; the last line is the
  signature line and nothing follows it.
- Cut candidates if long: line 10, half of line 19.
