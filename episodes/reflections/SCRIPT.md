# THREE SECONDS LATE: script (3 parts)

Dark-humor horror for TikTok, told as three 70–90 s parts. Each part opens on its single most important line with a
striking image in the first two seconds, and ends on a huge cliffhanger: a reveal or an A/B choice.

**Narrator:** KAI, 14, dry and funny even when he's terrified. Right-handed. That detail matters.
**Family:** MOM (practical, scared but holding it together), REMI (Kai's little brother, 7, asks the worst questions at
the worst times) and GRANDMA BEA (81, fearless, the funniest person in the house).

**The rules of this world** (the viewer learns them one at a time):
1. Every reflection on Earth is running a few seconds late, and the delay grows.
2. Anything that shows your face counts: mirrors, windows, puddles and black phone screens.
3. If your reflection ever moves *before* you do, it's ready to come out.
4. A broken mirror lets them out in pieces.
5. A reflection is backwards, so the copies use the wrong hand.

---

## PART 1: "IT MOVED FIRST" (about 80 s)

**HOOK (0–3 s).** Extreme close-up: Kai brushing his teeth. He stops. His reflection keeps brushing, slowly, staring at him.
On-screen card: **THREE SECONDS LATE · PART 1**

> **KAI (V.O.):** "At 3:07 a.m. on a Tuesday, every reflection on Earth fell three seconds behind."

**(3–8 s).** A globe; red dots spread city to city; a counter ticks: **3.0 s DELAY**.
> "By Friday, they'd stopped trying to catch up."

**(8–16 s).** A quick montage. People tape newspaper over mirrors, a store window full of shoppers whose reflections don't
move, and a puddle where a man's reflection looks UP at him.
> "Mirrors. Windows. Puddles. Your phone when the screen goes black. If it shows your face… it isn't on your side anymore."

**(16–24 s): dark humor.** Kai's dad (seen once, in a photo on the fridge with a band-aid collage on his face).
> "My dad said it was fake. Then he shaved without a mirror. Now he looks like he lost a fight with a blender, and he
> believes everything."

**(24–34 s).** A TV news anchor with a black bar where her studio monitor should be.
> **ANCHOR:** "The delay is growing. Three seconds. Then five. Then ten. And if your reflection ever moves *before* you
> do… do not look away."

Kai signs a school form at the kitchen table with his **right** hand, a quick, natural plant for the ending.
> **KAI:** "Cool. Normal Tuesday."

**(34–48 s).** The family covers every mirror in the house, with blankets and tape. Grandma Bea blocks her bedroom door.
> **GRANDMA:** "I've survived two husbands, a recession, and your mother's cooking. I am not scared of my own face."
> **REMI:** "What if your face is scared of *you*?"
> **GRANDMA:** "Then she's got good sense."

She keeps her big vanity mirror uncovered.

**(48–62 s).** Night, a dark hallway, rain. Kai's phone screen is black in his hand, and his reflection in it is late:
it lowers the phone a full breath after he does. He flips the phone face down.
From Grandma's room comes laughter, Grandma's warm cackle, and she's talking to someone.
> **GRANDMA (O.S.):** "Oh, you were always the pretty one."

**(62–80 s) CLIFFHANGER.** Kai pushes the door open. Grandma sits in her chair facing the vanity, with her back to us.
In the mirror, Grandma is **standing up**. She looks straight past the chair, at Kai, and **waves**.
> **KAI:** "Grandma… which one of you is talking?"

Both of them turn their heads at the same time. Only one of them blinks.
Card: **WHICH ONE IS REAL? · FOLLOW FOR PART 2**

## PART 2: "THE WRONG HAND" (about 85 s)

**HOOK (0–3 s).** Start on the mirror-Grandma's palm pressed against the glass, and the glass **bulging** outward like
water.
Card: **THREE SECONDS LATE · PART 2**
> **KAI (V.O.):** "My grandma had two of her. One in the chair. One in the mirror. And the one in the mirror was
> pushing."

**(3–14 s).** Kai throws a blanket over the vanity. *Knock. Knock. Knock*, from **inside** the glass.
> **GRANDMA (in the chair, calm):** "Let her out, baby. She's got better posture than me. She could do the dishes."
> **KAI:** "Grandma, NO."

**(14–26 s).** Morning, and the TV again: the delay is now **11 seconds** worldwide.
> **ANCHOR:** "Do not break a mirror. A broken mirror lets them out… in pieces."
> **KAI (V.O.):** "So that's what 'seven years of bad luck' meant. It was never luck. It's how long a thing takes to find
> all its pieces."

**(26–40 s): dark humor.** Breakfast. Grandma is extra sweet: she made pancakes, she hums, she even laughs at Remi's
jokes.
> **REMI:** "Why is Grandma being nice?"
> **MOM:** "Eat your pancakes."
> **KAI (V.O.):** "Grandma doesn't laugh at Remi's jokes. Nobody laughs at Remi's jokes."

**(40–55 s): the clue.** Close on Grandma lifting her coffee mug, with her **left** hand. Mom freezes. Kai sees Mom
freeze.
> **KAI (V.O.):** "Eighty-one years. My grandma has been right-handed for eighty-one years. Reflections are backwards."

Grandma notices them staring. She switches the mug to her right hand. Slowly. *Three seconds late.*

**(55–70 s).** Mom pulls Kai into Grandma's room and rips the blanket off the vanity. Inside the mirror is the REAL
Grandma, exhausted, her hair a mess, and she breathes on the inside of the glass and writes with her finger. The letters
are backwards: **PLEH**.
> **KAI (V.O.):** "The one we covered up last night… was the real one. And the one we let make breakfast…"

**(70–85 s) CLIFFHANGER.** From the kitchen comes humming, Grandma's song, the one she's hummed every night of Kai's life.
Except it's **backwards**. The humming stops, and a chair scrapes.
> **FAKE GRANDMA (O.S., sweet):** "Kai, honey? Who are you talking to?"

Split screen: **A: Break the mirror** and get the real Grandma out, though a broken mirror lets them out in pieces.
**B: Cover it again** and live with the one in the kitchen.
Card: **A OR B? WHAT DO YOU DO? · FOLLOW FOR PART 3**

## PART 3: "LIGHTS OUT" (about 90 s)

**HOOK (0–3 s).** Slow motion: a mirror **shattering**, and in every falling shard, a different face.
Card: **THREE SECONDS LATE · FINAL PART**
> **KAI (V.O.):** "We broke the mirror. Grandma came out. So did everything else."

**(3–15 s).** The real Grandma tumbles out onto the carpet, furious and alive. The shards on the floor **twitch**, each
one turning to look up at them.
> **GRANDMA:** "Took you long enough. I've been doing her chores for two days. Do you know how many dishes that woman
> uses?"

**(15–28 s).** In the kitchen doorway, Fake Grandma **ripples** like heat haze and smiles too wide.
> **KAI (V.O.):** "Rule we figured out the hard way: no light, no reflection. No reflection… no them."

Mom slams the breaker. **Total darkness.** The only sound is glass tapping on glass, everywhere at once.

**(28–42 s): dark humor in the dark.**
> **REMI (whispering):** "What if I need to pee?"
> **GRANDMA (whispering):** "Then you pee in the dark like your grandfather did. God rest his aim."

**(42–58 s).** Kai's phone lights up in his pocket with a notification. He grabs it to shut it off, and in the black
glass around the text he sees his reflection. It is **not** late anymore. **It's early.** It lifts a finger to its lips:
*shhh*, before Kai has moved at all.
> **KAI (V.O.):** "If your reflection ever moves first… don't look away."

He looks away.

**(58–72 s).** Darkness. Mom's voice comes from the left: "Kids, come to me." Then Mom's voice again, from the right:
"Kids, come to *me*."
> **KAI:** "Mom… which hand did you braid my hair with? Every morning. Which hand?"

Both voices answer at the same time: "My **right**, baby." Silence. Then one of them laughs, three seconds after the
other.
Kai **flips the breaker**. Light hits, and one Mom **ripples and flinches**. Grandma throws the blanket over it, and the
whole family **dumps it into the bathtub** with the shards and slams the bathroom door. Everyone is breathing hard.

**(72–80 s).** Morning. Sun. The TV says the delay is dropping worldwide: **0.4 s… 0.0 s.** Reflections back in sync.
Mom hugs both boys. Grandma eats pancakes with her **right** hand and complains about them.

**(80–90 s) FINAL TWIST.** Kai at the bathroom mirror brushing his teeth, the same shot as the very first frame. His
reflection copies him perfectly, with no delay. He puts down the toothbrush and signs the same school form from Part 1…
with his **left** hand.
> **KAI (V.O., smiling):** "Everything's back to normal now. My family's safe. My reflection copies me perfectly."
> (beat) "Honestly? I like it better out here."

His reflection in the mirror **bangs on the glass**, screaming silently. Kai calmly hangs a towel over it.
Card: **THREE SECONDS LATE · THE END?**

## Why the openings work

| Part | First line (said in the first 2–3 s) | First image | What it promises |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "At 3:07 a.m., every reflection on Earth fell three seconds behind." | A reflection that keeps brushing after he stops | One clear, creepy rule with worldwide scale |
| 2 | "My grandma had two of her… and the one in the mirror was pushing." | Glass bulging like water under a hand | Picks up mid-scare and answers part 1 with something worse |
| 3 | "We broke the mirror. Grandma came out. So did everything else." | A mirror shattering with a face in every shard | Answers the A/B choice and raises the stakes in one line |

Alternative first lines if you want to test variations:
- P1: "Your reflection is three seconds late. On day four, mine wasn't late anymore."
- P1: "Cover every mirror in your house. I'll explain why."
- P2: "Reflections are backwards. That's how we found out Grandma wasn't Grandma."
- P3: "No light, no reflection. So we turned off every light in the house. Then my mom said 'come here'… twice."

## Production notes (for when we animate)
- Characters: Kai, Mom, Remi, Grandma Bea, plus "copy" versions (the same look with a faint ripple and a too-wide smile).
- Sets: a bathroom mirror, Grandma's bedroom with the vanity, the kitchen, a dark hallway. Plus the globe and the TV
  news inserts.
- New effects needed: a reflection drawn in a mirror plane with a time delay (render the same character at T − delay,
  mirrored), glass ripple and bulge, shattering shards, and full darkness lit only by a phone.
- On-screen cards: the part title at the top, captions word by word, and an A/B choice screen at the end of part 2.
