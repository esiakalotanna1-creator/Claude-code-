# The Man at the Window v2 (YouTube cut, straight horror)

Source: the shot-by-shot brief in SPEC.md. Narrator: one take in a low, measured thriller voice (audio/narration_take.wav, not shipped).
No comedy flip, no jump scare, no lunge, no broken glass. Label: "AN INTERNET HORROR STORY" in the hook.

## Hooks considered
1. **"When I looked up... there was a man pressed against my window."** The viewer assumes he is outside, and the reel is built on that assumption. Picked: it is the brief's own line.
2. "A man was smiling at me through my own window." It shows the smile but loses the "pressed against" image.
3. "I had no idea how long he'd been there." A good second line, but too vague as an opener.

## Narration (79 words, 47 s spoken over 50.8 s)
When I looked up... there was a man pressed against my window. I had no idea how long he'd been there.
He wasn't knocking. He wasn't asking for anything. He just stood there... smiling through the glass.
My wife was asleep upstairs. Our little boy was in his crib. I wanted to shout... but couldn't. I stood frozen.
The gardening shears were still in my hand. Because I wasn't looking out of my house. I was looking in.
He was inside. With them.

## Retention edit
- The opening raises the question (who is he, why is he there). The second line develops it (how long?).
- Setup is short: the stare is the whole threat, and every 3-4 s brings a new picture (face, father, hand, family, mouth, slide, shears).
- Every beat adds a stake or a clue: the family upstairs, the frozen father, the shears, the reveal.
- The promise is kept: "my window" pays off when we learn which side of it the stranger is on.
- Nothing left to cut.
