# Script method (every script)

The brief is a 45-90 s reel told in an informative storytelling voice, with suspense, clear cause and effect,
and a satisfying ending. Aim for about 120-200 spoken words, then measure the real narration length.
A compelling story beats squeezing in every detail. **If the person gives you their own script or beat
sheet, use it verbatim.** Suggest changes as quoted before/after pairs and never rewrite it silently.

1. **Open with the most interesting contradiction.** The first sentence is specific and unsettling, and
   the story actually explains it ("When doctors removed this teenager's bandages, he smiled. His mother
   didn't."). Never open with a greeting, "Have you ever wondered" or "scariest story ever". The first
   0-3 s is the best-looking moving shot of the reel, paired with an intriguing spoken line (a
   character's own voice where possible).
2. **One main question**, raised through events. Never say "keep watching". If an identity is the final
   reveal, keep the name out of the narration, captions and early visuals.
3. **A chain of consequences.** Each event causes or explains the next. It is always clear who does what
   and why.
4. **Every beat changes something** every few seconds: new information, a decision, an escalation or a
   reaction. Let the big reveal breathe.
5. **Write for speech and animation.** Use conversational English and concrete verbs that land on the
   first listen. Every beat has something to show (a hand stopping at a door, a face changing). Keep
   the characters and locations few.
6. **Keep the middle moving.** Compress the background and cut repeats, filler and unneeded names.
7. **Earn the ending.** Answer the opening question, put the reveal right before the end with the
   signature line, and add no explanation afterwards and no "follow for more".
8. **Keep it honest.** Present creepypasta as a fictional internet story (an early "AN INTERNET HORROR
   STORY" label). Pick one version and stay consistent.
9. **Deliverable** `SCRIPT.md`: three distinct hooks and the pick with a one-line reason; the full
   narration; a timed shot table (narration, visible action, on-screen text, sound cues); the word count
   and estimated length.
10. **Retention edit.** Does the opening raise a specific question, and does the next sentence develop
    it? Is the setup shorter than the action? Does every beat add something? Can someone follow it
    without knowing the legend? Does the reveal arrive before the viewer feels strung along? Which
    sentence can go?

## Twist reels (creepy setup, crude comedic flip)

A creepy, played-straight setup flips into a crude comedic payoff before ~20 s, with the hero fighting back.
Land the flip with a music cut or a deep bass hit plus a push-in, and use jump cuts on the punch line.

## Captions

Captions back up the narration: 1-4 word chunks with one `[keyword]` highlighted. They never cover a face
or a hand.
