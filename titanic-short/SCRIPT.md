# The Unsinkable Ice Cube Tray (Titanic short) - DRAFT 1

## Facts (check status in brackets)
- Titanic hit an iceberg 14 Apr 1912, about 11:40 pm ship time, and sank about 2:20 am. [well documented]
- Lookout Frederick Fleet rang the bell and phoned the bridge: "Iceberg, right ahead." The ship turned but not in time. [US Senate Inquiry, 1912; National Geographic]
- The berg scraped the starboard side and opened the forward compartments; five of 16 flooded. Ship was designed to float with the first four flooded. [sources disagree on exact counts; wording kept loose]
- Bulkheads (the walls between compartments) only rose to E Deck, not to the top, so water spilled from one compartment into the next as the bow sank. [US Senate Inquiry; engineering write-ups]
- 1911 trade magazine The Shipbuilder called Titanic "practically unsinkable". [to verify before final]
- 20 lifeboats, room for 1,178, on a ship carrying about 2,200. Legal minimum then: 962. [from memory: VERIFY]
- 1914: first SOLAS convention (lifeboat seats for all) and International Ice Patrol founded. [from memory: VERIFY]
- Sister ship Olympic was refitted with taller bulkheads after the sinking. [from memory: VERIFY]
- Left out on purpose: the "forgotten binocular key" story. Sources say it is contested (the key may have opened a phone box, not a binocular locker).
- Analogy: ice cube tray. Fill one cup, tilt the tray, water spills into the next.

## Voice-over (~230 words, ~62 s at 3.7 words/s)
1. HOOK: The unsinkable Titanic was built like an ice cube tray.
2. SETTING: April 1912. The biggest ship in the world. Sixteen watertight compartments. One magazine even called her "practically unsinkable."
3. ACTION: Four days out, near midnight, a lookout shouts: "Iceberg, right ahead!" The ship turns. Slowly. Very slowly.
4. She doesn't crash head-on. She scrapes along the side, and the ice opens up the front compartments, one after another.
5. SNAP: She was built to float with four flooded. Five was one too many.
6. DEADPAN: Luckily, every compartment had a wall. Unluckily, the walls didn't reach the ceiling.
7. EXPLANATION: Fill one cup of an ice cube tray, then tilt it. The water doesn't stay put. It spills into the next cup, and the next. That is exactly what the sinking bow did, over the top of every wall.
8. CONSEQUENCE: Then came the lifeboats. Twenty boats, room for 1,178, on a ship carrying about 2,200. The law only asked for 962.
9. Two years later, the rules changed: a seat for everyone, and an ice patrol to watch the sea.
10. CALLBACK TWIST: Her sister ship got a rebuild, with taller walls. Your freezer's ice cube tray is still waiting for the update.
